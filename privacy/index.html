Jass Stübli – Privacy

November 2026

Deutsch

Jass Stübli does not collect any data. There is no account, no advertising, no tracking, no analytics and no server. What you do in the app stays on your device – unless you send something yourself: to other players near you or by email to us.

Responsible

Florian Bitterli, apps_bitterli@icloud.com

On your device

Settings, your player name, saved games, statistics and your learning progress stay on your iPhone or iPad. They are part of your device backup if you make one. If you delete the app, they are gone.

Playing together

The devices connect directly over the local network, not over the internet and through no server. Devices nearby see the name of the table and the name of the person who opened it. At the table, only the players' names and the moves are exchanged. The connection is encrypted with the table code.

Feedback and problem reports

Under Settings → Feedback and at the table ("Report a Problem") you can write us an email. The app only prepares it: you see everything in it, and you send it yourself. It contains

  • your text and your sender address,
  • the app version, the device model, the iOS version, the language and the chosen mat,
  • at the table, the running game as a file (rules, cards, moves); the other players' names in it are replaced by their seat.

The email goes through your email provider to our mailbox at iCloud Mail. We use it only to answer you and to fix problems, do not pass it on, and delete it once it is no longer needed for that. Legal basis, where the GDPR applies: your consent by sending it (Art. 6(1)(a)) and our legitimate interest in an app without errors (Art. 6(1)(f)).

Crash reports

If you allowed sharing analytics with app developers in iOS, Apple passes crash reports to us without information about you. You can change this under Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Your rights

You can ask about the emails you sent us and have them corrected or deleted: apps_bitterli@icloud.com. You can also complain to the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) or, if you live in the EU, to the data protection authority of your country.
